Built on piles sunk into the Thames.\n- **Notable Features**:\n  - Historic significance – Captain Cook drank here before his voyage to Australia.\n  - Samuel Pepys visited when buying cherries from an adjacent garden.\n  - Panoramic view of Tower Bridge from terrace.\n  - Located near remains of Edward III’s 14th-century manor house.\n\n---\n\n### **The Dog and Bell – Deptford**\n- **Address**: 116 Prince St, London SE8 3JD  \n- **History**: First established in 1741; currently on site since early 19th century (originally on Dog Street).\n- **Notable Features**:\n  - Thriving independent pub with a wide selection of guest beers.\n  - Bar billiard table, weekly trad jazz sessions on Wednesdays.\n  - Annual Pickle Festival in November.\n  - Listed as a Community Asset by Lewisham Council.\n\n---\n\n### **The Pilot – Greenwich**\n- **Address**: 68 River Way, Greenwich, London SE10 0BE  \n- **History**: Opened in 1801 as the “Pilot and Ferry Inn”; surrounded by marshes at time of opening.\n- **Notable Features**:\n  - Rare survivor on the Greenwich Peninsula.\n  - Adjacent cottages built for workers at tidal mill and chemical works (Ceylon Place).\n  - Now a Fuller’s pub with cozy interior and rear terrace.\n  - Near Millennium Dome/O2 Centre and modern apartment blocks.\n\n---\n\n### **The Trafalgar Tavern – Greenwich**\n- **Address**: Park Row, London SE10 9NW  \n- **History**: Built in 1837 on former fishermen’s pub site.\n- **Notable Features**:\n  - Regency-style architecture with maritime-themed artwork.\n  - Life-size statue of Admiral Horatio Nelson outside.\n  - Historic whitebait supper tradition from 19th century.\n  - Used as retirement home for seamen until 1965, then reopened as pub.\n\n---\n\n### **The Plume of Feathers – Greenwich**\n- **Address**: 19 Park Vista, London SE10 9LZ  \n- **History**: Oldest pub in Greenwich (established 1691), originally a coaching inn.\n- **Notable Features**:\n  - Located near major Greenwich attractions but off tourist trail.\n  - Traditional, local atmosphere; patrons know each other.\n  - Family-owned free house and restaurant.\n\n---\n\n## 🏙️ **Southwark / Central South London**\n\n### **The George Inn – Borough**\n- **Address**: 75 Borough High St, London SE1 1NH  \n- **History**: Last surviving galleried coaching inn in London;
